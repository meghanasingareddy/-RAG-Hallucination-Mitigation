The Solar System formed 4.6 billion years ago from the gravitational collapse of a giant interstellar molecular cloud.
The vast majority of the system's mass is in the Sun, with the majority of the remaining mass contained in Jupiter.
The four inner system planets—Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars—are terrestrial planets, being composed primarily of rock and metal.
The four giant planets of the outer system are substantially more massive than the terrestrials.
The two largest planets, Jupiter and Saturn, are gas giants, being composed mainly of hydrogen and helium.
The next two, Uranus and Neptune, are ice giants, being composed mostly of substances with relatively high melting points compared with hydrogen and helium, called volatiles.
Pluto was once considered a planet but is now classified as a dwarf planet.
Alpha Centauri is the closest star system to the Solar System, at 4.37 light-years away.
The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System, with the name describing the galaxy's appearance from Earth.
Water on Earth is essential to all known forms of life.
